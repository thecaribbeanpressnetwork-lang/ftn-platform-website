A missed target, a cash squeeze or a backlog tells you where the pressure is showing. To find out where it started, we sit with the people who own the business, the people who do the work and the people who rely on it. We follow decisions, handoffs and incentives until we have an explanation we can test.

Listen across the business
Sales sees growth. Dispatch sees more split deliveries. Finance sees overdue invoices. Each team is reporting a different part of the same problem.
Trace an order from promise to payment
Check whether the rush orders sales approves are leaving in multiple shipments, and whether finance waits for the last delivery before billing. If that pattern holds, higher sales could be tying up more cash.
Test one change
Pilot a shared delivery commitment for one product line. Track split shipments, time to invoice and time to payment before widening the change.

Political strategyEarn the mandate.
Deliver on it.

Country over colour. People over party.

Before a candidate can exercise authority, they have to earn it. We assess what voters want changed, why they may hesitate to trust a challenger and what would make the candidate a credible choice. That shapes the case for change, the commitments the campaign can defend under scrutiny and how the team puts those commitments to the public.

We prepare for the transition while the campaign is still underway. If voters grant the mandate, the incoming team should know which commitments it can act on first, what requires an appropriation or another agency’s cooperation, and who will be accountable for delivery.

Work out what would earn trust
Public agreement that a problem exists can conceal disagreement over the remedy. We test whether the proposed change addresses what voters care about, whether the candidate can defend the trade-offs and what would make the commitment credible before the election.
Map authority, resources and veto points
Trace the proposed commitment through the office, the budget holder and the agencies that would deliver it. Identify where approvals, procurement or inter-agency dependencies could change its cost, timing or scope.
Stress-test the transition into office
Use Gill Reasoning to record the assumptions behind the commitment and the evidence that would overturn them. Assign responsibility, set decision points and prepare a fallback if funding slips or a partner withdraws. Define how the office will report progress to residents.

In our exploratory research, ideas from quantum science help frame questions about observation and uncertainty. We test those ideas against evidence before drawing conclusions for a project.

Evidence-Bounded Retrodiction

Evidence-Bounded Retrodiction (EBR) is a method for reconstructing how an outcome came about. It distinguishes what was recorded at the time, what the people involved could have known then, and what later evidence helps explain. It preserves contradictions and leaves unresolved questions open.

We apply that discipline to real-world investigations and manifestation projects that turn an intention into a tangible result. In an investigation, it helps test competing explanations for what happened. In a manifestation project, it helps trace which decisions, actions and conditions moved the project toward its intended outcome, and which links still need evidence. The preprint explores how AI can support this reasoning.

Gill Reasoning Principles

Gill Reasoning brings a broad set of named principles into a practical sequence: define the intended outcome, trace what must be true, act on evidence and check what happens.

Observe
What happened, and what evidence supports it?
Trace
Which events, assumptions and dependencies connect to the outcome?
Test
What evidence would change the explanation or recommendation?

What this means for a long-term investment

For a project that depends on public contracts, start with three questions. Is funding approved for the full term? How long can the project carry delayed payments? What happens to the investment if refinancing costs rise? The debt figures give those questions context. The budget allocation, contract terms and cash-flow model help answer them.
