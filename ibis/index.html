HOW IBIS SHOULD THINK

Know what you know. Know what you inferred. Know the difference.

ibis is being developed around evidence rather than confident-sounding output. A useful intelligence system should be able to distinguish documented facts, reported claims, inference, prediction, contradiction and uncertainty; preserve provenance; expose material conflicts; and keep consequential actions under human control.

UNDERSTANDING WHY

What happened is only the beginning of the question.

Most AI systems are very good at describing the world as it appears now. ibis is also being developed around a harder question: Why is reality the way it is now?

If a decision was made years ago, today's evidence can help us understand the outcome—but it should not be silently pushed backward and treated as though the original decision-maker already knew it. A more honest reconstruction asks what information existed then, what alternatives appeared possible, what constraints applied, what choice was made and what happened afterward.

This research direction is formalized within ibis as Evidence-Bounded Retrodiction: separating what was known at the time, what can later be reconstructed about historical access to information, and what is known only in retrospect. It does not claim that chronology proves causation. Its purpose is to make historical reasoning more disciplined.

OBSERVER

Sometimes the important thing is the question you did not know to ask.

Traditional AI waits for a prompt. Observer is the proactive side of the ibis concept. With appropriate permissions, ibis can watch defined public or user-authorized signals and surface something when it becomes meaningfully relevant: new evidence, a public indicator, a deadline, a policy change, a local pattern or another condition that crosses a useful threshold.

The objective is not constant interruption. It is useful attention. A good observer should also know when nothing needs to be said.

DECISION INTELLIGENCE

Understand public reality without pretending to be government.

FTN's civic and scenario workspaces explore whether public information, community observations and transparent analytical models can help citizens, journalists, researchers, communities and institutions ask better questions about local reality—even when no government has formally adopted the platform.

What problems are appearing repeatedly? Where are they concentrated? What has already been tried? What evidence supported a decision? What changed afterward? What alternatives existed?

ibis does not need to be government in order to help people reason about governance.

HEADSPACE

See the whole picture without losing the thing you are working on.

Headspace is the spatial working concept behind ibis. Instead of treating intelligence as an endless vertical chat log, relevant evidence, alternatives, tools, decisions and active work can move into and out of focus while remaining part of a larger context.

The goal is simple: focus narrowly when necessary, pull back when relationships matter, and do not lose the larger picture because attention moved somewhere else.

THE LONG VIEW

Make the Caribbean computationally smaller without making it culturally smaller.

Different islands can remain different. Different institutions can remain independent. Different cultures can remain distinct. ibis is intended to understand the connections rather than erase the differences.

A Caribbean person should eventually be able to speak naturally, state what they are trying to accomplish, and have regional knowledge together with the best available technology organize itself around helping them.

If that architecture works in a region as fragmented and diverse as the Caribbean, the underlying approach may also be useful anywhere people live among disconnected systems that were never designed to work together.
