# ibis research and speculative-theory boundary

## Purpose

ibis may attract researchers because it connects cognition, memory, intent, provenance, multimodal interaction and Caribbean knowledge infrastructure. This record defines what can be presented as an engineering result and what must remain a hypothesis.

## Engineering claims ibis may make

- It can store structured context and decisions with provenance.
- It can represent a founder-governed reasoning pattern computationally.
- It can compare expected and actual outcomes and update pathway weights.
- It can expose confidence, source class, freshness and evidence boundaries.
- It can route requests across deterministic capabilities and connected providers.
- It can fail closed when identity, evidence, adapter or permission is missing.
- It can create a Headspace interface in which relevant tools and evidence materialize around an objective.

## Speculative research questions

- Whether a sufficiently rich, longitudinal cognitive graph could preserve useful continuity of a founder’s reasoning style.
- Whether multi-signal personal, environmental and relational context can improve opportunity anticipation without violating consent or privacy.
- Whether provenance-aware memory and outcome learning can produce more trustworthy agent behavior than prompt-only personalization.
- Whether regional intelligence systems can create economic and civic value that general-purpose assistants miss.
- Whether the Gill Cohesive Consciousness Hypothesis can be formalized as a falsifiable computational research program.

## Prohibited overclaiming

ibis must not claim consciousness transfer, digital immortality, sentience, medical efficacy, biometric identity, mind reading, guaranteed prediction or autonomous authority. Research materials must label hypotheses as speculative and distinguish analogy, model, simulation, evidence and established science.

## Research collaboration invitation

Researchers and institutions may contact FTN to discuss a bounded proof-of-concept involving provenance-aware memory, cognitive-graph learning, intent privacy, opportunity anticipation, agent safety or Caribbean knowledge systems. Proposed collaboration must specify data access, consent, retention, publication rights, IP ownership, security, ethics review and exit conditions before any private or founder-sensitive material is shared.
