Cohesion is economic infrastructure.

The Caribbean does not only need more information. It needs more organized information. Organization improves discovery. Discovery creates connections. Connections create transactions. Transactions create income. Income creates capacity. Capacity makes businesses, communities, cities and institutions more resilient and more strategic.

Why is Face The Nation building FTN Index?

FTN stands for Face The Nation. FTN is an idea. It began in San Fernando, Trinidad and Tobago, with a simple proposition: the Caribbean becomes stronger when we organize what we already have — businesses, opportunities, institutions, communities, culture, public information and knowledge — and connect those pieces so they create more value together.

A Caribbean business may exist mainly on Instagram, Facebook or WhatsApp. A contractor may be trusted throughout one community and almost invisible outside it. A student can miss a bursary. A small business can miss a tender. A musician can have listeners across the world without a coherent professional identity. An investor can underestimate a city because nobody has assembled a clear picture of what is actually operating there.

The International Labour Organization reported in 2025 that approximately three in every four Caribbean workers — about 5.85 million out of a 7.8 million workforce — are engaged in informal employment. That does not mean three quarters of Caribbean GDP is informal. It shows the scale of economic participation that can be difficult to represent, connect, finance and scale through conventional systems.

Source: International Labour Organization →

If we do not organize Caribbean information, someone else eventually will. FTN believes the region should have a representative say in how Caribbean businesses, communities, institutions, culture and opportunities are described and discovered by people, search engines, AI systems, investors and institutions — and should participate in the value created from that representation.

What is FTN Index?

FTN Index identifies publicly available information associated with Caribbean businesses and organizations and presents it in a clearer, standardized form. Depending on the business and the public information available, that can include name, business type, location, address, phone, public email, website, opening hours, services, booking links and platform-specific social identities such as Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, X, YouTube and LinkedIn.

If a field says [Not found], FTN did not locate that item in the public sources reviewed. It does not mean the information does not exist. Businesses can correct or add factual information about themselves free.

Why did FTN contact my business?

While building FTN Index, an automated discovery process identified publicly available information associated with your business. The verification invitation lets you see what FTN found, correct what is wrong and add what is missing. There is no charge to review, correct or confirm factual business information.

This outreach is generated through a vibe-coded AI aggregation and discovery agent built in San Fernando, Trinidad and Tobago. Its purpose is to help find Caribbean businesses that can be fragmented, underrepresented or difficult to discover online. The automated agent does not make FTN an authority over a business; it helps surface public information for review.

What is the economic opportunity?

UN Data lists the Caribbean at approximately 44.622 million people, about US$554.864 billion in GDP and about US$12,793 GDP per person in its regional table. Small improvements applied to an economy of that scale become large numbers.

Source: United Nations Data →

The Inter-American Development Bank has separately estimated that closing Caribbean digital-access gaps could increase regional GDP by about 6% to 12% over the medium term. The IDB also reports an association between a 10 percentage-point improvement in digital infrastructure and approximately 3.2% higher GDP and 2.6% higher productivity over six years. This is not an FTN forecast; it demonstrates why digital infrastructure that reduces friction can have macroeconomic consequences.

Source: Inter-American Development Bank →

Why does FTN refer to the golden ratio?

The golden ratio is the mathematical proportion φ ≈ 1.6180339. Its reciprocal is approximately 0.6180339. FTN uses the relationship as a design and systems-thinking motif for propagation, diminishing downstream effects and compounding — not as a claim that geometry itself causes economic growth.

Reference: Encyclopaedia Britannica →

FTN sometimes uses 0.618 as an explicitly labeled scenario convention: Bₙ = B₀ × 0.618ⁿ. The useful question is what happens after the first-order result. Better discovery can create a transaction; a transaction can create income; income can finance capacity; capacity can create employment; employment can support another business. The thesis is compounding, not magic.

What is ibis-ai?

ibis-ai is FTN's developing Caribbean intelligence layer. The aim is not a generic chatbot with a Caribbean skin. The goal is intelligence that can work across Caribbean entities, statistics, communities, institutions, opportunities, culture, evidence and language context — including FTN's work around Caribbean lexical patterns relevant to Creole and Patois identification.

The architecture is deliberately connected: public Caribbean information → FTN Index → source and confirmation structure → FTN APIs → ibis-ai → Caribbean answers, analysis and decision support.

FTN's long-term intention is that core access to useful Caribbean public knowledge should remain broadly available to Caribbean people, while sustainable commercial products can be built from the structuring of public information into APIs, institutional analysis and decision-making tools for the betterment of the Caribbean.

Why include side hustles, family businesses and informal businesses?

Because economic reality does not begin when somebody incorporates a company. Many Caribbean people earn through a personal side hustle, self-employment, a microbusiness or a family enterprise with little formal digital infrastructure. FTN's role is not tax enforcement. Better visibility can simply move someone along a voluntary path: findable → contactable → credible → earning → growing → formalizing, if and when the owner chooses.

Will FTN ask whether a business is registered?

It may be offered as an optional question during business review. FTN should not infer that a business is unregistered merely because a registration record was not found. A voluntarily supplied registration-status answer is private by default and should not automatically appear on the public record, public API or AI response. FTN is not building an enforcement or tax-liability database.

Does it cost anything to correct my information?

No. Businesses do not pay FTN to review, correct or confirm factual public information about themselves. FTN Index is not intended to make factual correction a pay-to-play feature.

What does the FTN confirmation mark mean?

It means the public information associated with the business was recently confirmed through the FTN Index process. It is a freshness signal — not an endorsement, safety certification, quality guarantee, tax-compliance statement or recommendation.

Is FTN affiliated with Google, OpenAI, Gemini, Perplexity or another technology company?

No affiliation should be inferred. FTN may name major search and AI services because they make the changing information-discovery environment easier to understand. FTN does not represent those companies or imply their endorsement.

Is FTN being paid or sponsored to build FTN Index?

FTN is not currently presenting FTN Index as a sponsored programme. If a future investment, sponsorship or commercial partnership materially affects how information is presented, FTN should disclose that relationship clearly where relevant.

Does FTN sell information about my business?

FTN Index is built from public information. FTN does not need to turn confidential information privately supplied by a business into a hidden product. FTN may build sustainable commercial products from the structuring of public information into APIs, analysis and decision-support tools. That is different from quietly selling private information supplied in confidence.

Is FTN a large company?

No. FTN is an idea being built into working infrastructure from San Fernando, Trinidad and Tobago. The ambition is large; the operation is lean. What matters is whether the systems work, whether the information can be inspected and improved, and whether the ecosystem creates useful Caribbean capability over time.
