Cohesion is economic infrastructure.

The Caribbean does not only need more information. It needs more organized information. Organization improves discovery; discovery creates connections; connections create transactions; transactions create income; income creates capacity; and capacity creates stronger businesses, communities and institutions.

Why is Face The Nation building FTN Index?

FTN stands for Face The Nation. FTN is an idea. The idea is that the Caribbean becomes stronger when we organize what we already have: our businesses, opportunities, institutions, communities, culture, public information and knowledge.

A business can exist mainly on Instagram or WhatsApp. A contractor can be well known locally and invisible outside one community. A student can miss a bursary. A small company can miss a tender. A musician can have global listeners but no coherent professional identity. An investor can fail to see what exists in a Caribbean city because the information is scattered across dozens of public sources.

The International Labour Organization reported in 2025 that approximately three in every four Caribbean workers — about 5.85 million out of a 7.8 million workforce — are engaged in informal employment. That does not mean three quarters of Caribbean GDP is informal; it shows the scale of economic participation that can be difficult to represent, connect, finance and scale through conventional systems.

Source: International Labour Organization →

If we do not organize Caribbean information, someone else eventually will. Face The Nation believes the Caribbean should have a representative say in how the region is described, discovered and understood by search engines, AI systems, investors, institutions and the wider world — and should participate in the economic value created from that information.

What is the economic opportunity?

UN Data lists the Caribbean at approximately 44.622 million people, about US$554.864 billion in GDP and about US$12,793 GDP per person in its current regional table. Small improvements applied to an economy of that scale become large numbers.

Source: United Nations Data →

The Inter-American Development Bank has separately estimated that closing Caribbean digital-access gaps could increase regional GDP by about 6% to 12% over the medium term, and reports an association between a 10 percentage-point improvement in digital infrastructure and approximately 3.2% higher GDP and 2.6% higher productivity over six years. This is not an FTN forecast; it demonstrates why digital infrastructure can have macroeconomic consequences.

Source: Inter-American Development Bank →

Why does FTN use the golden spiral?

The golden ratio is the mathematical proportion φ ≈ 1.6180339. Its reciprocal is approximately 0.6180339. FTN uses the spiral as a visual and systems-thinking motif for compounding relationships — not as a claim that the golden ratio itself causes economic growth.

Reference: Encyclopaedia Britannica →

FTN sometimes uses 0.618 as an explicitly labeled scenario convention for diminishing downstream effects: Bₙ = B₀ × 0.618ⁿ. The question is what happens after the first result: discovery can create a transaction; a transaction can create income; income can finance capacity; capacity can create employment; and employment can support another business. The thesis is compounding, not magic.

How do the FTN nodes connect?

FTN Platform is not intended to be a bag of unrelated apps. Each node addresses a different form of Caribbean information, discovery, culture or coordination friction.

FTN Community Connect + Mayor Mode

Community Connect turns reports about roads, flooding, waste, lighting, safety and infrastructure into structured civic information. Mayor Mode is the strategic municipal-intelligence view: maps, reports, demographics, indicators and correlations designed to help city leadership see patterns and make better local decisions. Until the public Mayor route is production-ready, this card intentionally opens Community Connect rather than a broken demo.

FTN Opportunities

One discovery layer for Caribbean jobs, grants, tenders, bursaries, scholarships, fellowships, accelerators, competitions, artist calls, training and funding programmes. An opportunity somebody never finds has zero economic value to that person.

FTN Statistics

Source-linked Caribbean indicators with evidence and mathematics in view, so important regional numbers can be inspected instead of merely repeated.

FTN Live

Situational intelligence across weather, transport, marine, environment, safety, infrastructure and civic conditions: what is happening around us now?

FTN Invest-in

Connects place, sector, data and opportunity so investors can move from country to city to community to evidence and see both existing strengths and investable gaps.

FTN Riddim + Caribbean music infrastructure

Riddim, Fire, DJ Tube, DAW, EPK and Radio form a Caribbean music stack spanning creation, production, performance, professional artist identity, discovery and audience reach. Caribbean music already creates global cultural and economic value; FTN's thesis is that more of the infrastructure around that value can be Caribbean-built and connected.

FTN Screen

Caribbean film, filmmaker and screen-work discovery, helping regional stories, rights-aware metadata, trailers, festival pathways and professional submissions become easier to find and organize.

FTN Kaiso

Caribbean journalism and public-information discovery with credible perspectives and original sources in view. Information quality is part of economic and civic infrastructure.

FTN Index

The entity and knowledge layer: clearer identity, public contact, services, social identities, sources, confirmation and machine readability for Caribbean businesses and organizations.

ibis-ai

The developing Caribbean intelligence layer connecting entities, statistics, opportunities, civic information, evidence and Caribbean language context into useful answers and decision support.

What is ibis-ai?

ibis-ai is FTN's developing Caribbean intelligence layer. The objective is not a generic chatbot with Caribbean branding. It is an intelligence capability that can increasingly work with Caribbean entities, statistics, institutions, communities, opportunities, culture, evidence and language context.

FTN has been developing Caribbean lexical-pattern capabilities relevant to Creole and Patois identification, Caribbean voice identities and evidence-aware statistical querying. Language carries culture and context. The long-term relationship is: public Caribbean information → FTN Index → sources and confirmation → FTN APIs → ibis-ai → Caribbean answers and decision support.

The intended principle is that useful core access to Caribbean public knowledge remains broadly accessible to Caribbean people, while sustainable commercial products can be built from the structuring of public information into APIs, analysis and decision-making tools for the betterment of the Caribbean.

Why include side hustles and informal businesses?

Because economic reality does not begin when somebody incorporates a company. Many Caribbean people earn through a personal side hustle, self-employment, a family enterprise or a microbusiness with very little formal digital infrastructure. Better visibility can support a voluntary progression: findable → contactable → credible → earning → growing → formalizing, if and when the owner chooses.

FTN is not a tax authority. Registration status, if requested in the review process, is optional and private by default. FTN should not infer that a business is unregistered merely because no registration record was found, and FTN does not intend to publish a list of businesses that say they are unregistered.

Is FTN a large company?

No. FTN is an idea being built into working infrastructure from San Fernando, Trinidad and Tobago. The ambition is regional; the operation is lean. FTN does not need to pretend to be a multinational institution for the idea to matter.

Is FTN paid, sponsored, affiliated with Google/OpenAI, or selling private business information?

No affiliation with Google, OpenAI, Gemini, Perplexity or another technology company should be inferred. FTN is not currently presenting FTN Index as a sponsored programme. If a material sponsorship, investment or commercial relationship becomes relevant, it should be disclosed clearly.

FTN Index uses public information. FTN does not need to sell confidential information supplied privately by a business. Sustainable products may instead be built from the structuring of public information into APIs, analysis and decision-making tools for the betterment of the Caribbean.
