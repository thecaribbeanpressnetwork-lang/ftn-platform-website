Frequently asked questions

Why is Face The Nation building FTN Index?

FTN stands for Face The Nation. FTN is an idea.

The idea is that the Caribbean becomes stronger when we organize what we already have: our businesses, opportunities, institutions, communities, culture, public information and knowledge.

The Caribbean does not lack talent, economic activity or cultural influence. What is often missing is cohesion. A business can exist mainly on Instagram or WhatsApp. A contractor can be well known in one community and invisible outside it. A student can miss a bursary. A small company can miss a tender. An artist can have a global audience but no coherent professional identity. An investor can fail to see what exists in a city because the information is scattered across dozens of public sources.

The International Labour Organization reported in 2025 that approximately three in every four Caribbean workers — about 5.85 million people out of a 7.8 million workforce — are engaged in informal employment. That does not mean three quarters of Caribbean GDP is informal. It does show the scale of economic activity that can be difficult to represent, finance, connect and scale through conventional systems. Source: International Labour Organization

FTN Index is intended to help shore up that information foundation. Better visibility can make legitimate economic activity easier to discover. Better structured information can make opportunities easier to match. Better regional intelligence can make investment and public decisions better informed.

If we do not organize Caribbean information, someone else eventually will. Face The Nation believes the Caribbean should have a representative say in how the region is described, discovered and understood on the planet — and should participate in the economic value created from that information.

What is the economic opportunity?

United Nations data estimates the Caribbean at approximately 44.6 million people and about US$554.9 billion in annual GDP for 2025, or roughly US$12,793 per person. Source: United Nations Data

Small percentage improvements applied to a regional economy of that size become large numbers. A hypothetical 0.1% improvement in economic activity equals about US$555 million. A 1% improvement is about US$5.55 billion.

The Inter-American Development Bank has separately estimated that closing Caribbean digital-access gaps could raise regional GDP by roughly 6% to 12% over the medium term. That is not an FTN forecast and it does not mean FTN Index alone would create such growth. It demonstrates that digital infrastructure can have macroeconomic consequences. Source: Inter-American Development Bank

FTN uses 0.618 in some scenario thinking as a deliberate golden-ratio-inspired modeling convention for downstream effects. It is not presented as an economic law.

A simple FTN propagation model can be written as Bₙ = B₀ × 0.618ⁿ, where a first-order benefit creates smaller second-, third- and later-order effects. The purpose is to force us to ask what happens after the first result: discovery can create a transaction; a transaction can create income; income can finance capacity; capacity can create employment; employment can increase household spending; and that spending can support another business.

The thesis is compounding, not magic. Organize the information. Connect the people. Reveal the opportunities. Build the intelligence. Compound the value.

What is FTN Index?

FTN Index is a Caribbean-first public-information standardization and discovery layer. It helps represent businesses and organizations with clearer identity, location, public contact information, services, opening hours, websites, social identities, booking links, sources and confirmation freshness.

It is not intended to be merely another business directory. The larger purpose is to make Caribbean entities easier for people, search engines, mapping systems, developers, AI services and FTN's own intelligence systems to understand.

What is the larger FTN Platform?

FTN Platform is Face The Nation's connected Caribbean digital ecosystem. Each module addresses a different kind of information or coordination friction, but the long-term value comes from the modules working together.

FTN Community Connect turns citizen reports about roads, flooding, waste, lighting, safety and infrastructure into structured civic information that communities and institutions can act on.

FTN Live brings situational information — weather, transport, marine, environmental, safety, infrastructure and civic conditions — into one public-interest view of what is happening now.

FTN Statistics organizes source-linked Caribbean statistics and exposes the evidence and mathematics behind the numbers so claims can be inspected instead of merely repeated.

FTN Opportunities is designed to bring jobs, grants, tenders, bursaries, scholarships, fellowships, competitions, accelerators, funding programmes, artist calls, training programmes and other opportunities into one discovery layer. An opportunity someone never finds has zero value to that person.

FTN Invest-in connects data, place, sector and opportunity so investors can move from country to city to community to evidence and see what exists, what is changing and where gaps may create opportunity.

FTN Govern and FTN Parliament make public institutions, representation and authoritative governance information easier to understand and navigate.

FTN Kaiso treats Caribbean journalism and public information as infrastructure: making multiple credible perspectives easier to discover while linking back to original publishers.

FTN Riddim, Fire, Radio, DJ Tube, DAW, EPK and Screen form parts of a Caribbean creator and media stack: creation, production, DJ tools, artist identity, electronic press kits, radio, visual distribution and audience reach. Caribbean culture already creates global economic value; FTN's thesis is that more of the infrastructure around that value can be Caribbean-built and connected.

Face The Nation is the public conversation layer from which the name FTN originated: the nation seeing itself, discussing itself and understanding itself.

FTN Index becomes the entity and knowledge layer connecting many of these experiences, while ibis-ai is being developed as the intelligence layer above them.

What is ibis-ai?

ibis-ai is FTN's developing Caribbean intelligence layer. The aim is not to build a generic chatbot with Caribbean branding. The aim is to make structured Caribbean information useful for answers, analysis, recommendations and decision support.

The long-term relationship is: public Caribbean information → FTN Index → sources and confirmation → FTN APIs → ibis-ai → Caribbean answers and decision support.

FTN has also been developing Caribbean language-identification capabilities around lexical patterns relevant to Creole and Patois, alongside Caribbean voice identities and evidence-aware statistical querying. Language carries culture and context. A Caribbean intelligence system should progressively understand not only what Caribbean people say, but how we say it and what we mean in context.

The intended beneficiaries include citizens, small businesses, creators, tourists, investors, journalists, researchers, municipalities, ministries and Caribbean governments.

The long-term principle is that core access to useful Caribbean public knowledge should remain broadly accessible to Caribbean people, while sustainable commercial products can be built from the structuring of public information into analysis and decision-making tools for the betterment of the Caribbean.

Why include small and informal businesses?

Because economic reality does not begin when someone incorporates a company. Many Caribbean people earn income through a personal side business, self-employment, a family enterprise or a microbusiness that may have little formal digital infrastructure.

Visibility can be one step in a larger progression: findable → contactable → credible → earning → growing → formalizing, if and when the owner chooses.

FTN is not a tax authority and does not determine whether someone should register or what taxes they owe. FTN's role is information, discovery and connectivity.

Will FTN ask if a business is registered?

FTN may provide an optional field allowing a business to indicate its registration status. FTN should not infer that a business is unregistered merely because no registration was found.

Registration status supplied through the FTN review process is intended to be private by default and should not automatically become part of the public business record. A business may separately choose whether it wants that fact made public.

FTN does not intend to create a public list of businesses that say they are unregistered. The purpose is to understand economic barriers and support better regional development, not to create an enforcement database.

Why did FTN contact my business?

While building FTN Index, FTN identified publicly available information associated with your business and is asking you to review what was found. There is no charge to correct or confirm factual information.

Where did FTN get my information?

FTN Index works with information that is already publicly available, which can include business websites, public social-media pages, maps, directories, government and institutional sources and other publicly accessible sources.

What does [Not found] mean?

It means FTN did not locate that field in the public sources reviewed. It does not mean the information or account does not exist. Businesses can add or correct missing information.

Why show Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, X, YouTube and LinkedIn separately?

For many Caribbean businesses, social media is the primary digital presence. FTN treats each platform as a separate public identity so incorrect associations, missing accounts and outdated handles can be corrected precisely.

How do I correct my information?

A verification invitation opens a pre-filled FTN Index review page for that business. You can correct what is wrong, add what is missing and confirm the result. You do not need to buy a listing or subscription to correct factual information. You may also contact FTN at facethenationtt@gmail.com.

Does it cost anything?

No. Businesses are not charged to review, correct or confirm factual information about themselves in FTN Index.

Does FTN sell my information?

FTN Index is built from public information and does not need private business information in order to index public facts. FTN does not intend to sell confidential information supplied privately by a business. FTN may in the future commercialize APIs, analytics, institutional services and decision-support products built from the structuring and analysis of public information.

Is FTN being paid or sponsored to contact businesses?

No. FTN is not currently being paid by Google, OpenAI, Microsoft, Meta, Perplexity or another search or AI company to contact businesses for FTN Index. If a future sponsorship, investment or commercial relationship materially affects how information is presented, FTN's policy is to disclose that relationship clearly.

Is FTN affiliated with Google, ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity?

No. These services are examples of how search and information discovery are changing. FTN Index is an independent Face The Nation / FTN Platform initiative and does not imply endorsement or affiliation with those companies.

What does the FTN confirmation mark mean?

It means information associated with the entity was recently confirmed through the FTN Index process. It is a freshness and confirmation signal, not an endorsement, safety certification, quality guarantee, business-registration confirmation or tax-compliance statement.

What is FTN's ultimate ambition for the Caribbean?

FTN's ambition is larger than software. The north star is to help increase Caribbean capability, wealth and quality of life by making regional information, opportunity and intelligence more connected and useful.

Why should the Caribbean not aspire to become one of the most prosperous, connected and happiest regions per capita on Earth?

Economic growth should ultimately improve lived experience: opportunity, safety, community, mobility, culture, time, health, institutional quality, environmental resilience, belonging and personal prosperity.

Cohesion is economic infrastructure. United, the Caribbean can present a clearer picture of itself to the world, negotiate from a stronger information position and have a more representative say in the technologies, markets and decisions shaping the planet.

Is FTN a large company?

No. FTN is an idea being built into working infrastructure. It began with a Caribbean founder working from San Fernando, Trinidad and Tobago, asking what Caribbean-first digital infrastructure could look like if modern technology were deliberately connected around regional needs.

FTN does not want to pretend to be a giant institution. The ambition can be large while the operation remains lean. What matters is whether the systems work, whether the information can be trusted and whether the ecosystem becomes more useful over time.
