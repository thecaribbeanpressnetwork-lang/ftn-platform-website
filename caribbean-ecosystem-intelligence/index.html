Who This Is For

Founders, researchers, journalists and institutions navigating a Caribbean sector.

A civic-tech founder trying to find the shortest credible path to funding. A researcher trying to understand who funds Caribbean startups and why the connections are weak. A journalist trying to map the organizations behind a public program. An institution trying to see where its own ecosystem has gaps. All of them currently do this by opening a dozen browser tabs and building a mental map by hand.

The Problem

Caribbean ecosystem information is real, but fragmented across hundreds of separate sources.

The organizations, funders, programs and relationships that make up a Caribbean sector genuinely exist -- but no single, current, trustworthy map of them exists anywhere. A funder's own website says nothing about who else funds the same space. A startup accelerator's page does not mention which regional development bank actually backs its cohorts. The connections have to be reconstructed by hand, every time, by every person who needs them.

Generic search returns pages. It does not return a map, and it has no way to tell you when a connection is genuinely unverified versus confidently known.

How FTN ibis Solves It

Ask a plain question. Get a sourced map, not a list of links.

FTN ibis's EcoMap reasoning connects three things for one objective: place (where an organization or resource actually sits), pathway (the real steps/requirements to reach it) and relationship (who is genuinely connected to whom, and how). Where live search grounding is available, the answer is built from real, cited, retrieved sources -- shown alongside the answer, never hidden. Where evidence is genuinely thin, ibis says so honestly instead of inventing a confident-looking map.

This is not a static database of Caribbean organizations FTN curated once and let go stale. It is a live reasoning process that runs fresh each time you ask, grounded in current search results when the question calls for them.

Frequently Asked

Common questions about Caribbean ecosystem intelligence.

What is Caribbean ecosystem intelligence?

It is the practice of mapping the real organizations, funding pathways and relationships that make up a Caribbean sector -- civic technology, culture, business or public life -- so a founder, researcher or institution can see who actually influences an ecosystem and where the credible paths through it run, instead of guessing from scattered websites.

How is this different from a directory or a search engine?

A directory lists organizations one at a time with no relationships between them. A search engine returns pages, not a map. FTN ibis's EcoMap reasoning connects place, pathway and relationship together for one objective, cites its real sources, and honestly says when it does not have enough evidence rather than guessing.

Is the data real or illustrative?

When ibis has live source grounding for a question, its answer is built from real, cited, retrieved sources shown alongside the response -- never invented. Where evidence is genuinely unavailable, ibis says so honestly instead of fabricating a plausible-looking map.

Is this free to try?

Yes. Ask FTN ibis directly at ftnplatform.org/ibis-ai/ -- no account or payment is required to ask an ecosystem-mapping question.
