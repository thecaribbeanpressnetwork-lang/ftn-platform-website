ibis

Continuity is a capability.

A conventional chatbot can answer a question well and still lose the larger story. ibis is being developed around the opposite requirement: maintain useful continuity across projects and time, while still focusing tightly on the problem in front of it.

The goal is not to make memory larger. It is to make memory more useful to reasoning.

That means representing not only facts, but decisions, assumptions, alternatives, reasoning paths, outcomes and changes in belief. It also means knowing when a stored item is evidence, when it is inference, and when uncertainty should remain unresolved.

Applied capabilities under development include longitudinal context, causal and backward reasoning, opportunity identification, structured memory, personalized decision support and learning from prior reasoning paths rather than simply accumulating conversation text.

Persistent cognitive architecture

Preserve the path to a decision, not only the decision.

The Founder Cognitive Layer is a persistent architecture concept for recording how consequential decisions evolve: the alternatives considered, assumptions in force, evidence available, causal pathways proposed, outcomes observed and lessons carried forward.

The practical research question is whether persistent agents can develop better continuity by retaining structured decision history and belief change instead of repeatedly rebuilding context from a shallow transcript. Speculative ideas about deeper cognitive preservation are deliberately outside the claims made on this page.

Working paper · persistent agents

From Memory to Causal History: Evidence-Bounded Retrodiction for Persistent Agents

Persistent AI systems need a disciplined way to reason about the past. The central problem is simple: once an agent learns what eventually happened, it becomes easy to describe an earlier decision as though later information had already been available. This work explores how an agent can reconstruct candidate causal histories while keeping different kinds of historical knowledge separate.

1 · Historical record

What was documented or known at the time a decision was made?

2 · Access evidence

What later evidence supports that an actor actually had access to particular information at that time?

3 · Retrospective causal history

What can later evidence support about the mechanism and path that produced the outcome?

Research themes include persistent agent memory, causal retrodiction, temporal provenance, epistemic time, mechanism-gated causal explanation, contradiction preservation, open-set uncertainty and abstention when evidence is insufficient. The paper is active research; this page does not claim acceptance or publication.

Problems I want to solve

Concepts worth testing.

Not every useful problem should be presented as a shipped startup. These are product concepts being explored because the underlying user problem is concrete and the design constraints are meaningful.

Concept

Damaged-screen phone recovery interface

A legitimate owner connects a functioning phone with a damaged display to a computer, mirrors or interacts with the device, enters the phone’s normal password and retrieves personal data.

Security boundary: this concept is not a phone-unlocking or authentication-bypass product. The owner still supplies the device’s existing credentials.

Concept

Personal photo recovery & family archive

A tool to scan fragmented personal archives across old phones, drives and computers, identify a specified family member across different ages, and consolidate matching photographs into a durable archive.

The preferred direction emphasizes family-memory preservation, explicit owner control and local/private processing where feasible.
