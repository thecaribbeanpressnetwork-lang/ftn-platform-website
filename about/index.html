About FTN Platform

One region. Many systems. One connected layer.

FTN Platform is an independent Caribbean digital ecosystem built to make the region easier to understand, navigate and connect.

Our Mission

Make Caribbean complexity easier to understand and act on.

Across the Caribbean, useful information, public services, community knowledge, culture, media, opportunities, data and digital tools exist across thousands of separate places. The problem is often not that nothing exists. The problem is that everything exists apart.

FTN connects those signals around what people are actually trying to accomplish. A person should not need to know which agency, database, website, media outlet, application or digital system contains the next piece of what they need.

Different FTN products provide different doorways, but they belong to one connected system. One ecosystem. Many ways in.

Our Vision

Make the Caribbean computationally smaller.

The Caribbean consists of many countries, territories, governments, currencies, institutions, cultures, datasets and service systems. They do not need to become identical for the region to become easier to navigate.

FTN's long-term vision is a connective intelligence layer in which existing systems can remain independent while becoming easier for people to understand and use together. This is not political centralisation and it is not an attempt to replace existing institutions. It is interoperability from the person's point of view.

A citizen should not have to understand the architecture underneath the region simply to function effectively within it.

Why FTN Exists

Because fragmentation is a solvable problem.

FTN began with civic information, community reporting and public affairs, but the same pattern kept appearing elsewhere. Community problems were disconnected from public information. News was disconnected from underlying evidence. Opportunities were disconnected from people who might qualify for them. Cultural creators used separate systems for every part of their work. Regional information lived inside individual countries, institutions and platforms.

Building another isolated application for every problem would reproduce the fragmentation. So FTN became an ecosystem.

Community Connect, FTN Live, Parliament, Kaiso, Face The Nation, Opportunities, Riddim, TV, Screen and the other FTN surfaces each have a specific job. FTN ibis is being developed as the intelligence layer capable of understanding the relationships between them.

Independent by Design

Useful public intelligence should not depend on institutional permission to exist.

FTN Platform is independent. It is not a government website, and the presence of government information or civic tools does not imply endorsement, partnership or adoption by any government or public agency unless FTN explicitly says so.

Public records, authoritative sources, community-contributed information and other appropriately obtained data can already help people understand their environment more clearly. A community can ask what is happening. A journalist can inspect the evidence. A researcher can examine patterns. A citizen can understand a public decision. An institution can use the same underlying reality if it chooses to engage.

The information should not change depending on who is looking at it.

The Caribbean Reality

What is real matters—even when conventional systems do not measure it well.

Caribbean life is larger than formal institutions and formal datasets. Vendors, tradespeople, creators, freelancers, family businesses, independent workers, community networks and countless small forms of economic and social activity shape the region every day.

FTN should be able to understand that reality without turning people into surveillance products. Over time, appropriately public, licensed, aggregated or consented signals may help reveal where people repeatedly encounter friction, where needs are concentrated, where capabilities exist, and where the official picture differs from lived experience.

Private individual information should never become the price of public insight.

Caribbean Knowledge

Our region should be represented as it actually speaks, thinks and lives.

Caribbean people do not communicate in one standardized voice. Our accents, dialects, Creoles, vocabulary, cadence, code-switching, place names, cultural references and ways of expressing meaning vary across countries and communities.

FTN and ibis therefore treat Caribbean-native language and contextual knowledge as important infrastructure. Where such resources are developed or incorporated, the objective is to work from authoritative public sources, properly licensed material, research partnerships and genuinely consented contributions, with provenance and rights attached.

The goal is not to manufacture one generic “Caribbean voice.” It is to help technology understand the diversity that is actually here.

Founding Principles

What we hold ourselves to.

1

Reality First

Start with what can be observed and supported, not the narrative we would prefer to be true.

2

Provenance Matters

Important information should retain its source, time, context and uncertainty wherever practical.

3

Human Agency

Technology should expand people's ability to understand and act, not quietly take control away from them.

4

Connect, Don't Flatten

Regional connection should preserve local differences, jurisdictions, cultures and source identities rather than erase them.

5

Build for Continuity

Products can change. The underlying mission, evidence and institutional memory should survive them.

Our Philosophy

Reality is the final authority.

An FTN system should not begin by deciding what story it wants the data to tell. It should observe reality, identify what is known, distinguish evidence from assumption, expose uncertainty and revise its understanding when better evidence appears.

  • Reality is the final authority. Every observation is a clue.
  • Every relationship is a hypothesis. Every intervention is an experiment.
  • Every outcome becomes new evidence. Better decisions should leave the system wiser than before.
  • To understand where we are, understand how we got here. Historical decisions should be judged using the information and constraints that actually existed at the time, not rewritten by hindsight.
Connecting Without Centralising

One Caribbean does not have to mean one database.

FTN's objective is not to pull everything into one giant uncontrolled repository. Sources should retain their identity. Jurisdictions should remain visible. Provenance should remain attached. Conflicting information should be allowed to remain conflicting until evidence resolves it.

The connective layer should understand relationships without erasing difference. That is how FTN can become regional infrastructure while remaining trustworthy.

The Long View

Built to still matter when today's technology has changed.

Technology will change. AI providers will change. Interfaces will change. Individual products will evolve. The underlying mission should survive all of them: help Caribbean people understand their reality, connect what is fragmented, preserve what is trustworthy and turn better understanding into better action.
